**Intro**

Before we begin, I want you to think about the last meal you ate. How much food did you waste? Now think of the store owners. Unsold food and vegetable trimmings are thrown away at the end of each day. Our project seeks to give new value to this abundant food waste. 

**Problem**

In **2025**, annual food waste increased approximately **4.6% in two years** with **790 tonnes of food waste in 2025 \[NEA\]**. **Food-waste recycling rate** also remained **stagnant at 18% \[NEA\]**.

As such, there is an urgent need to reduce food wastage in Singapore.

When surveying current measures to combat food waste, our group found out about BugBoom. BugBoom is an alumni-run passion project turned business that started in NTU. This company uses black soldier flies to process food waste, turning the larvae into when live feed, and waste into fertilizer. 

**Food Waste in NTU**

Wanting to improve on the foundation set by BugBoom, we surveyed campus and we realised that BugBoom would not work in a campus setting as students are not fond of insects. We also found that most food was contaminated with plastic and other non recyclables (Refer to lukas picture).

**Solution**

Our project revolves around students taking ownership of food waste recycling by being able to visualise its effects. This brings us to Bokashi Bros, a student-led food recovery system that uses Bokashi fermentation to turn separated canteen food waste into compost that can be returned to NTU’s grounds. (Show bokashi) 

**First**, a hands-on process by stakeholders is needed to inculcate values of responsibility and accountability, so they understand the impact of their actions.

Store owners should place uncooked or unsold food into a composting bin, where EM Bokash is used to quicken the fermenting process.

The poster you received contains instructions for students. Their job is to separate food waste from other potential contaminants. Club members would check to ensure proper sorting.

**Second**, we propose setting up a club in NTU that oversees this initiative. While we will elaborate more later, club members have 2 responsibilities. First, they are to ensure that sorting has been properly done by store owners and students. Second, they transport the waste to partnering stakeholders for treatment using Bokashi. These stakeholders include gardening halls like Hall 12, or external bodies like **Foodscape Collective and Project Black Gold** at Boon Lay Nature Garden.

**Third**, Bokashi produces both compost and liquid byproducts. The compost will be used as fertilizer in halls or by external bodies, while the liquid can be used as a chemical-free drain cleaner to clean drains in NTU in a more eco-friendly manner.

Through this, we hope that at every step, stakeholders are able to feel the impact that they can make through reducing food waste. 

Earlier, we suggested that a club oversees this initiative. We hope for this club to inculcate the value of accountability amongst members. Creating a student body that understands the importance and need for a proper annual hanover so that completing the loop is more than an individual effort.

The club benefits include awarding hall points for student dormitory allocations as well as carbon credits that help to reduce students' groceries expenditures.

We will measure the how successful this project is at the end of the semester once we have piloted our project through two main ways:

1) At least 50% participation rate among store owners.   
2) The main student body proactively sorts their food waste, reducing the need for club members to actively ensure compliance.
